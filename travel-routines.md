*A study of how members of online travel communities manage ongoing personal routines away from home*

**Ethics review reference:** ERN_6967-Nov2026  
**Version:** 1.1, 4 November 2026

> **In brief**
> - We are reading and analysing public posts in which travellers describe keeping up an ongoing personal routine while away from home.
> - We do not contact anyone, and we never publish screen names, links or wording that could be traced back to a post.
> - If you would like your posts to be excluded, email [j.liao@bham.ac.uk](mailto:j.liao@bham.ac.uk) by 30 June 2027. No reason or proof is needed.

## About this page

This page explains a research study at the University of Birmingham that analyses publicly available posts from online travel communities. If you have posted in one of these communities, it explains what we do with posts, how we protect the people who wrote them, and how to ask for your posts to be excluded.

## Who is carrying out the study

The study is led by Dr Jiancai Liao, Assistant Professor in Marketing at Birmingham Business School, University of Birmingham, with Songyin Cai, also of Birmingham Business School. The University of Birmingham is responsible for the study. The study is not funded by any external organisation.

## What the study is about

Many people travel while keeping up a routine that cannot simply be paused for the length of a trip: taking a regular medication, using a piece of personal equipment every night, keeping to a schedule of appointments, or renewing a supply at fixed intervals. At home such a routine is supported by familiar arrangements. On a trip, the traveller has to carry it along and keep it going alone.

Members of online travel communities often write about how they manage this. The study looks at what travellers do to keep such routines going away from home. We hope the findings will help travel and transport providers, tourism destinations and online communities understand this work and reduce the burden it places on travellers.

## Which posts we look at

- Posts in English that anyone can read without logging in, posted between 2009 and 2026.
- Posts written in the first person about the writer’s own routine in connection with a trip, including a decision not to travel.
- In total, around 500 to 600 posts drawn from several online communities.
- We describe these communities by type and do not name them, here or in anything we publish. Naming them would make it easier to trace a reworded quotation back to the original post.

We exclude posts about another person’s routine, posts from anyone who indicates they are under 18, and posts that express serious personal distress. Excluded posts are not analysed or quoted.

## What we do not do

- We do not post, reply, send messages or take part in any community.
- We do not read private messages, members-only areas or profile pages.
- We do not collect posts in any way a site’s terms of use do not permit.
- We do not seek medical details, and we do not analyse anyone’s health.

## How we protect the people who wrote the posts

- No screen names, links to posts, profile details or locations below country level appear in anything we publish or share.
- Every quotation we publish is reworded so that it keeps the writer’s meaning but cannot be found by pasting it into a search engine.
- Where a combination of details could single someone out, we generalise the details or do not quote the passage.
- The information needed to find a post again (the link to the post and, until 30 June 2027, the screen name) is kept in a restricted file on University storage that only the lead investigator can open. In the working copy of the data, screen names are replaced by codes.

## Use of AI tools

To help screen and organise posts, pseudonymised text, with screen names and links removed, may be processed with a generative AI tool approved by the University, in line with the University’s AI policies and data protection safeguards. The text is not used to train AI models. Every output is checked by a researcher, and all decisions about the analysis are made by the researchers.

## How to ask for your posts to be excluded

Email the lead investigator at [j.liao@bham.ac.uk](mailto:j.liao@bham.ac.uk). Please give the name of the community and your screen name or a link to the post. You do not need to give a reason or prove that the posts are yours. We will remove all material that matches the details you give, and reply to confirm that any matching material has been excluded. To protect other people’s privacy, our reply will not say whether we held any material linked to those details.

Requests received on or before 30 June 2027 will result in your posts being removed from the study. After that date the analysis will have been completed and it will not be feasible to remove individual contributions; because no published output contains any quotation that can be traced to a writer, this would not change what a reader could learn about you. If you delete a post from its site before the analysis is completed, we will treat it as withdrawn and remove it.

## Your information and your rights

The University of Birmingham is the data controller for this study. The posts we analyse can contain personal information, such as a screen name and what a person has written about themselves. Some posts may mention health. We treat all of this as sensitive personal information and apply the safeguards described above.

The University processes this information because it is necessary for research carried out in the public interest (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(e) and Article 9(2)(j)), with the safeguards required by the Data Protection Act 2018. No decision or action concerning any individual will be taken on the basis of the research.

**Storage and retention.** All data are held on secure University storage and are not shared publicly. Screen names will be deleted on 30 June 2027, once the deadline for exclusion requests has passed. Links to posts, kept so that we can show a journal or the University that the material is genuine if asked, will be deleted by 30 November 2030. The pseudonymised data, which contain no screen names or links, will be kept in the University’s Research Data Store for ten years after the study ends, as University policy requires, and then securely deleted.

**Your rights.** You can ask to see the information we hold about you, ask for your posts to be excluded, or object to their use. Some rights may be limited where information is used for research, but we will always honour exclusion requests received by 30 June 2027. For questions about how the University handles personal data, contact the University’s Data Protection Officer at [dataprotection@contacts.bham.ac.uk](mailto:dataprotection@contacts.bham.ac.uk). You also have the right to complain to the [Information Commissioner’s Office](https://ico.org.uk).
